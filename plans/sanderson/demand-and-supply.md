# Automating Daily Work — Demand and Supply

This document analyzes Steve's daily work using Tony Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI) framework. The **demand side** identifies core functional jobs (solution-independent, long-lived) and desired outcomes. The **supply side** maps current solutions to the jobs and outcomes they serve, and identifies the consumption chain jobs — the solution-specific overhead of managing those solutions.

---

## Core Functional Jobs

### Job 1: Secure new professional engagements that match my practice

Build out private practice ("Alright. What's next?") by finding and pursuing opportunities for interim, fractional, or advisory CPO / CTO / CPTO engagements.

This job is long-lived and solution-independent. It persists regardless of whether the solution involves job boards, colleague referrals, inbound leads, or something else entirely.

#### Job Map

1. Determine the right kind of engagement **[define]**
2. Find potential opportunities from multiple sources **[locate]**
3. Research an opportunity to decide if it's worth pursuing **[prepare]**
4. Decide to pursue and set up a project to track it **[confirm]**
5. Actively pursue the engagement **[execute]**
6. Track where each active pursuit stands **[monitor]**
7. Adjust approach based on what's happening **[modify]**
8. Close out a pursuit **[conclude]**

#### Desired Outcomes

##### Step 2: Find potential opportunities from multiple sources [locate]

| # | Desired Outcome |
|---|---|
| 2.1 | Minimize the time it takes to identify a relevant opportunity |
| 2.2 | Minimize the likelihood of missing a relevant opportunity that matches my criteria |
| 2.3 | Minimize the effort required to scan multiple sources for opportunities |
| 2.4 | Minimize the likelihood that an opportunity expires before I notice it |
| 2.5 | Minimize the time it takes to determine whether an opportunity matches my criteria |
| 2.6 | Increase the quality of opportunities surfaced (fit to practice, likelihood of converting) |
| 2.7 | Increase the likelihood that opportunities come pre-qualified through a trusted relationship |
| 2.8 | Increase the likelihood that the hiring party has prior awareness of my credibility when I engage |

### Job 2: Maintain my professional network so it stays active and generates opportunities

Keep relationships alive across both the *personal network* (people known directly through professional relationships) and the *brand network* (people connected through the work itself — community, content, reputation). The desired outcome is that the network generates awareness, referrals, and opportunities over time.

This distinction (personal vs. brand network) comes from a conversation with Greg Bear about how he maintains his network across gregslist and Practical Founder.

### Consumption Chain Jobs

These are solution-specific activities performed daily in service of the core functional jobs. They exist because of the specific solutions in use — they are the overhead of managing those solutions. Organized by function, not by time-of-day.

#### Set the daily agenda

Review actions to surface what must happen today:
- Review any action waiting on someone else — if the waiting period has expired, surface it as something to act on today.
- Review any action scheduled for today, or past due, and surface it for today.

This is typically the first activity of the day. Serves all core jobs.

#### Triage incoming communications

Process inputs from multiple channels so nothing important falls through the cracks. Each item gets classified, a decision is made on action, and it gets routed to the right project/context. Serves Job 1 (a recruiter emails) and Job 2 (a contact reaches out).

Channels:

| Channel | Notes |
|---|---|
| Email inbox | Goal: inbox zero |
| Apple iMessages | |
| Slack | |
| WhatsApp | |
| Discord | |
| LinkedIn notifications | |
| MyChart | Medical |

Rules vary by source and sender. For example:
- A direct email from a known contact → connect to an existing project, follow up immediately or create an action to follow up.
- A direct email from an unknown sender → get background on them and their organization, scan the email, decide on action.
- A newsletter (e.g., Whole Dog Journal) → quick scan, decide whether to save as reference.
- A LinkedIn notification from someone being "watched" → create a follow-up action to reach out with something personal, with the goal of maintaining/increasing their awareness.

The desired outcome is a system that learns these rules over time and applies them with increasing accuracy.

#### Manual scan of job boards

Check job boards daily for opportunities worth pursuing. Primarily serves Job 1.

Sources: FractionalJobs.io, ExecThread, GLG.

When an opportunity passes the relevance bar, create a project to track the application process.

#### Organize actions into projects

Triage actions that haven't been assigned to a project. Assign them to the right project context and set attributes (e.g., cost of delay, stakes of not finishing). The goal is that all actions are correctly set up so they appear in the right context for prioritization. Serves all core jobs.

Currently managed in Tana.

#### Decide what to work on next

Review actions across active projects (excluding those that are "waiting" or "scheduled" — those are handled by the daily agenda). Use attributes like cost of delay and stakes to decide what to work on next. This is not a hard commitment — it's more "consider this as next." This question repeats throughout the day whenever it's time to pick up something new. It's the GTD question: "what should I do next?" Serves all core jobs.

Currently managed in Tana.

### Possible Solutions (addressing underserved outcomes)

The outcome satisfaction assessment above shows most outcomes for Job 1 Step 2 are underserved (high importance, low satisfaction). The following solutions are hypothesized to better satisfy those outcomes.

#### MCP as a solution

MCP servers could provide the assistant with access to external services, enabling it to perform consumption chain jobs on Steve's behalf.

| Use Case | Underserved Outcomes Addressed | Core Job |
|---|---|---|
| Automated scan of LinkedIn notifications to surface relevant activity | 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4 (time, coverage, effort, timeliness) | Job 1, Job 2 |
| Automated review of LinkedIn network suggestions to flag people of interest | 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 (time, coverage, effort) | Job 1, Job 2 |
| Fill out background info on a contact and their organization | 2.5, 2.6 (matching speed, quality) | Job 1, Job 2 |
| Migrate projects and related content from Tana into DXOS Composer | Enables Composer as central hub (see below) | All core jobs |

#### DXOS Composer as a solution

Composer could serve as the central hub for managing daily work, replacing or augmenting current tools.

**Incoming message triggers:** Composer could trigger actions when new messages arrive from external sources, replacing the manual triage of multiple channels. This requires interfaces to:
- Email
- Apple iMessages
- WhatsApp
- Slack
- Discord
- LinkedIn notifications
- MyChart

This would shift "triage incoming communications" from a manual daily sweep across many apps to an event-driven system where items arrive in Composer and get processed there. Directly addresses underserved outcomes around timeliness (2.4) and effort (2.3) — items would surface without manual scanning.

**Automated / augmented rule learning:** Composer (through some combination of plugins, prompts, blueprints, and/or markdown documents) could learn and apply the rules for what to do with each incoming item — increasing the speed and consistency of triage over time. For example:
- Recognizing a sender and auto-linking to the right project
- Suggesting an action based on past patterns for similar items
- Applying known rules (e.g., "Whole Dog Journal → reference only") without prompting

Addresses underserved outcomes around matching speed (2.5) and quality (2.6). Also reduces the likelihood of inconsistent execution (2.2, 2.7) by automating what is currently done manually and sporadically.

# MCP Plugin — Consumption Chain Analysis

The remainder of this document analyzes one specific supply-side solution — the Composer MCP plugin — as a consumption chain within itself. This analysis was the original content of this document and is preserved below for reference.

---

## Why MCP Plugin Configuration Isn't a Core Functional Job

A core functional job is the primary task a user is trying to accomplish — the reason they "hired" the product in the first place. Examples of core functional jobs that Composer users perform:

- **Research a topic** — gather, synthesize, and organize information
- **Analyze a dataset** — explore data, identify patterns, draw conclusions
- **Write a document** — draft, edit, and refine written content
- **Manage a project** — track tasks, coordinate work, make decisions
- **Build software** — design, implement, test, and deploy code

MCP server configuration is not any of these jobs. Nobody opens Composer thinking "I need to configure an MCP server." Instead, MCP servers provide *tools* that help the AI assistant perform these core functional jobs better — a web scraping tool helps with research, a database tool helps with analysis, a code execution tool helps with software development.

The plugin itself is infrastructure. It doesn't do the user's work — it extends the assistant's ability to do the user's work. This places it squarely in the **consumption chain**: the set of jobs users must perform to acquire, set up, use, and maintain a product so it can help them with their core functional jobs.

---

## Consumption Chain Job Statement

> **Extend the AI assistant with external tool capabilities so it can perform specialized work on my behalf.**

This is a consumption chain job because:
1. It exists only in service of core functional jobs (research, analysis, writing, etc.)
2. Nobody wants to do it for its own sake — it's overhead
3. Its value is measured by how well it enables the core jobs, not by the configuration activity itself

---

## Where This Fits in Ulwick's Consumption Chain Taxonomy

Ulwick defines [consumption chain jobs](https://www.marketingjournal.org/how-to-engineer-micro-moments-using-jobs-to-be-done-anthony-ulwick/) as the activities customers perform *around* a product — not the primary reason they purchased it. The canonical types are:

| Consumption Chain Job | Definition |
|---|---|
| Purchase | Decide to acquire the product |
| Receive | Obtain the product after purchase |
| Install & set up | Prepare the product for use |
| Learn to use | Acquire knowledge to operate the product |
| Interface with | Ongoing interaction with the product |
| Transport | Move the product |
| Clean | Maintain product cleanliness |
| Store | Organize and keep the product |
| Maintain | Routine upkeep |
| Upgrade | Enhance or improve the product |
| Repair | Fix broken or degraded function |
| Dispose | Remove the product at end-of-life |

**The MCP plugin maps primarily to "Install & set up" and "Maintain"** — with elements of "Learn to use" and "Repair":

- **Install & set up**: Discovering, configuring, and connecting MCP servers so the assistant gains new tool capabilities. This is the dominant job.
- **Maintain**: Updating server configurations as URLs change, servers are added/removed, or needs evolve.
- **Learn to use**: Understanding what tools a configured server provides and how they become available in conversations (via blueprints).
- **Repair**: Diagnosing and resolving connection failures when a configured server becomes unreachable.

The "product" being installed/maintained is not the plugin itself — it's the *AI assistant's extended capability*. The plugin is the mechanism through which the user performs these consumption chain jobs.

## Implications for the Supply Side

The demand-side analysis reveals several things about the current spec:

1. **The spec covers Steps 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 well.** Configuration, enablement, use, update, and removal are the core loop — and the requirements address them.

2. **Step 4 (Validate) is the most significant gap within scope.** A user configures a server and has no way to know it works until they start a conversation and either see tools or get a failure toast. A "test connection" action and/or tool listing would directly serve outcomes 4.1 and 4.3.

3. **Step 7 (Monitor) is partially served but reactive.** The user only learns about failures during conversation turns. There's no proactive monitoring, and failure diagnostics are minimal.

4. **Steps 1 and 2 (Identify need, Find tool source) are intentionally unaddressed.** This is appropriate for v1 — but they represent the largest underserved area in the consumption chain. A future MCP server registry or tool catalog would serve these.

5. **Outcome 9.3 (accidental removal) is a minor gap.** A confirmation dialog before deletion would address it at low cost.

These gaps don't necessarily mean the spec should change — some are intentionally deferred. But they should be visible as *known underserved outcomes* so the implementer and product owner can make informed scope decisions.
